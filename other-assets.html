ASSET GUIDE · EVERYTHING ELSE

Boats, equipment, and other big-ticket purchases

Outside of cars and real estate, the pattern is the same: cash removes the credit question entirely, and financing depends on whether the LLC has built any credit history of its own yet.

TWO ROUTES

Cash, or building the entity's own credit

The cash route

The LLC is the buyer on the purchase agreement, payment comes from the business account, and title or bill of sale is issued to the LLC. This is the most straightforward way to keep a new or thin-file entity out of a lending conversation entirely — there's no loan to underwrite.

The business-credit route

If you'd rather finance through the entity without a personal guarantee, that credit has to be built deliberately, and it typically takes one to three years of consistent activity before lenders treat the LLC as a borrower in its own right.

BUILDING BUSINESS CREDIT

What "seasoning" the entity actually looks like

Register with a business credit bureau

A business identification number with a commercial credit bureau is what lenders check instead of your personal score once the entity has one.

Open vendor trade lines

Net-30 vendor accounts and business credit cards that report to commercial bureaus — not consumer ones — are how an LLC starts building a payment history.

Keep every payment inside the business account

Mixing personal and business funds is the single fastest way to undo the separation you're trying to build — see the liability section for why that matters.

Give it time and volume

Lenders typically want to see a couple of years of tax returns and a reasonable volume of revenue moving through the business account before offering financing with no personal guarantee attached.

ASSET-SPECIFIC NOTES

A few things that change by category

Asset typeWhat's different
Boats & watercraftRegistration runs through your state's marine or wildlife agency rather than the DMV, and commercial marine insurance has its own underwriting separate from auto policies.
Equipment & machineryOften leased rather than purchased outright — leasing preserves cash flow and can be easier to qualify for on a thinner credit file than a purchase loan.
Aircraft & aircraft sharesTitled through the FAA registry; lenders in this space almost always want a personal guarantee regardless of entity age, given the size of the asset.
Inventory & collectiblesUsually just a bill of sale to the LLC, but insurance and storage need to be structured as business expenses to keep the paper trail clean.
